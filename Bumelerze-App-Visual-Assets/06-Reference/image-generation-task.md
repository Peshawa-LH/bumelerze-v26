# TASK: Illustration set for the Bumelerze earthquake app (22 images)

> **How to use this document (note to the human operator):** hand this ENTIRE file to an
> image-generation AI (paste it, or upload it as an attachment). It is fully
> self-contained — the AI needs no other context. If the tool can only take one prompt
> at a time, first give it Part 1 (the Style Bible) as a standing system/style
> instruction, then feed it the numbered image prompts from Parts 2 and 3 one by one.
> When the images come back, check them against Part 5 before accepting.

---

## Your commission

You are producing the complete illustration set for **Bumelerze** (Kurdish *bûmelerze* =
"earthquake"), a public-safety earthquake app for the **Kurdistan Region of Iraq**. After
an earthquake, users open the app — sometimes seconds after the shaking stops — and
report what they felt by **tapping a picture**, not reading text. Your images ARE the
interface: a Kurdish grandmother who reads no English must be able to look at a tile and
recognize "that is what just happened to me."

**Deliverables — 22 images, one consistent visual world:**

- **Set 1 (12 images):** the intensity scale — one scene per level of the European
  Macroseismic Scale (EMS-98), from "not felt at all" (I) to "total destruction" (XII).
  These show *people experiencing shaking*.
- **Set 2 (10 images):** the damage scale — two building types (a multi-storey
  apartment block and a single-storey house) × five damage grades each (DG1 "no
  damage" → DG5 "partial collapse"). These show *a building's condition*, exterior
  view, building as the subject.

All 22 must look like panels from **one single Kurdish animated production** — same
style, same palette, same recurring characters, same world. That consistency is the
hardest and most important requirement of this commission.

---

## Part 1 — Style Bible (applies to every image; treat as a standing instruction)

### 1.1 Art style

- **Flat 2D cartoon illustration** in the spirit of a warm, modern animated
  film/children's-book illustration — think contemporary Middle Eastern animation
  aesthetics, NOT western clip-art, NOT anime, NOT photorealism, NOT 3D render, NOT
  painterly/airbrush.
- **Thick, clean, dark outlines** and **limited flat color fills**. No gradients, no
  drop shadows, no texture noise. Every image must still read clearly when shrunk to a
  **120×120 pixel tile on a cheap phone screen** — one clear focal action per image,
  strong silhouettes, no background clutter competing with the main subject.
- **Warm, earthy palette**, held constant across all 22 images: ochres, terracotta,
  warm sand and stone tones for buildings; deep greens and blues as accents; skin,
  clothing and interiors from one small fixed set of muted warm-neutral colors. The
  finished grid of tiles must read as one coherent product, not 22 unrelated stock
  images.

### 1.2 The world: Kurdistan, drawn with love

Every scene is set in the **Kurdistan Region of Iraq, present day**. This must be
visibly, recognizably Kurdistan — not a generic Middle East, and absolutely never a
US/European suburb:

- **Buildings:** reinforced-concrete frame construction with brick/block infill walls,
  or load-bearing masonry houses — always with **flat roofs**. Rooftop water tanks,
  satellite dishes, external AC units, courtyard walls with metal gates. Never a
  pitched-roof wood-frame house, never a clip-art cottage.
- **Landscape:** the Zagros foothills — dry golden-brown hills in summer light — behind
  the towns; poplar trees; in wider scenes a distant skyline touch such as a minaret or
  a citadel silhouette reminiscent of Erbil's.
- **Interiors:** carpets and floor cushions, a wall hanging, tea served in small
  waisted glasses (*istikan*) on a tray, a glass-front dish cabinet — real Kurdish
  domestic detail, not generic Western furniture.
- **People — a recurring cast, mixed dress:** design a small cast of **4-5 recurring
  characters** and reuse them across the whole set (do not invent new-looking people
  for each image):
  - an older man in **traditional Kurdish men's dress** (baggy trousers with wide
    fabric waist sash — *shal û shapik* style — and a turban-style headwrap),
  - a woman in a **colorful traditional Kurdish dress** (long, with shimmering
    fabric),
  - a younger woman in contemporary clothing **without** headscarf,
  - a man in contemporary clothing (shirt/trousers),
  - a child.

  Women appear both with and without headscarf across the set; ages are mixed. This
  mirrors real Kurdish streets and homes.

### 1.3 Tone and safety — absolute rules

- **Non-graphic at every level, including the worst.** Damage happens to buildings and
  objects — cracked walls, fallen plaster, rubble, collapsed floors — **never to
  people. No injuries, no bodies, no blood, ever.** In high-intensity scenes people
  are shown from behind, at a distance, or in composed/alert posture; faces show
  concentration or mild concern, never anguish, terror or pain. The overall tone at
  high levels is *documentary and restrained*, like a serious news illustration — not
  a disaster-movie poster.
- **No fire, no smoke, no flames anywhere** (dust haze is allowed where a prompt asks
  for it).
- **No text of any kind in the image** — no numerals, letters, logos, signage with
  readable words, or watermarks. The app draws its own labels in four languages
  underneath each tile; baked-in text would be wrong in three of them.

### 1.4 Consistency workflow (recommended)

1. First generate a **character/style reference sheet**: the 4-5 recurring cast members
   standing together, plus one apartment block and one flat-roofed house, in the final
   style and palette.
2. Then generate the 22 images **using that sheet as the style/character reference**
   (via your tool's reference-image, seed, or style-consistency feature).
3. Generate each image **large (at least 1024×1024)** — never at final tile size.

## Part 2 — Set 1: the twelve intensity scenes

Each prompt below describes one scene. The *"Meaning"* line is the scientific definition
the scene encodes (from the official EMS-98 scale) — it is context for you, not text to
render. Apply the full Style Bible to every prompt.

### Image 1 of 12 — "Not felt" (EMS I)
*Meaning: not felt by anyone, even under the most favourable circumstances.*

A calm, ordinary Kurdish domestic interior, warm evening light. A woman sits reading on
a sofa. A full istikan tea glass rests undisturbed on a tray beside her. The ceiling
lamp hangs perfectly motionless. A cat sleeps curled on the carpet. Nothing in the
scene suggests any motion at all — total stillness and calm.

### Image 2 of 12 — "Barely felt" (EMS II)
*Meaning: felt only by very few individual people at rest indoors.*

A dim night bedroom in a Kurdish apartment. One person lies in bed with eyes open, a
small subtle "?" of quiet noticing — not alarm, just awareness. A hanging light cord
sways almost imperceptibly (a single thin motion line). A second person in the same
room sleeps completely undisturbed. Everything else is still.

### Image 3 of 12 — "Weak shaking" (EMS III)
*Meaning: felt indoors by a few; people at rest feel a light trembling.*

A daytime Kurdish living room. The older man in traditional Kurdish dress, seated,
notices a light tremble — the tea in his istikan glass shows rippling concentric lines,
and the ceiling lamp swings in small gentle arcs. Through the window behind him, a
passer-by on the street is visibly unbothered, noticing nothing.

### Image 4 of 12 — "Felt by many" (EMS IV)
*Meaning: felt indoors by many; windows, doors and dishes rattle; a few sleepers wake.*

An evening family room in a concrete-frame apartment — carpet and wall hanging visible.
A three-generation family in mixed traditional and modern dress all look up at once,
alert. The glass-front dish cabinet rattles (small motion lines around the dishes).
Window panes show faint vibration lines. A child stirs awake on the couch. Nothing has
fallen or broken.

### Image 5 of 12 — "Strong shaking" (EMS V)
*Meaning: felt by most; sleepers wake; small objects topple; doors swing open.*

The same style of apartment interior, now visibly agitated: the ceiling lamp swings in
a wide arc, a framed picture tilts off-level, tea glasses slide across a low table and
one small ornament topples off a shelf mid-fall, a door swings open on its own. A
startled woman steadies herself with one hand on the table edge; another person sits
bolt upright in bed. The building itself is completely undamaged.

### Image 6 of 12 — "Light damage" (EMS VI)
*Meaning: many people frightened, run outdoors; slight non-structural damage — hairline
cracks, small pieces of plaster fall.*

A split indoor/outdoor scene: people hurry calmly but urgently out of a flat-roofed
masonry house's front door onto the street — composed urgency, not panicked faces.
Through the open doorway, books and jars have fallen off shelves, and one wall shows a
fine hairline crack in the plaster with a few small flakes on the floor beneath it. The
building's exterior walls and roofline are fully intact. Golden Zagros hills in the
distance.

### Image 7 of 12 — "Damaging" (EMS VII)
*Meaning: most people run outdoors; furniture shifts; moderate damage — cracked walls,
fallen plaster, parts of parapets fall.*

Street view of a two-storey concrete-frame house with block infill walls, Kurdish
townscape. A small group of people stand gathered at a safe distance, watching. Visible
diagonal cracks run across one infill wall panel; chunks of fallen plaster and a broken
piece of rooftop parapet lie at the building's base; the rooftop water tank sits
visibly tilted. A small inset shows the interior: a toppled wardrobe and shelf contents
spilled across the floor.

### Image 8 of 12 — "Heavy damage" (EMS VIII)
*Meaning: many find it difficult to stand; large wall cracks; weak older buildings may
partially collapse.*

A dusty street scene (light dust haze, no smoke). In the foreground the older man in
traditional Kurdish dress braces one hand against a low wall to keep his footing —
knees bent, wide stance, expression of concentrated effort, not fear. Behind him an
older masonry house has a partially collapsed corner with large X-shaped cracks in the
remaining wall; a neighbouring concrete-frame building stands upright but its infill
panels have fallen away, exposing the bare frame. A rubble pile sits on the ground with
no people near it.

### Image 9 of 12 — "Destructive" (EMS IX)
*Meaning: many weak buildings collapse; even well-built buildings show very heavy
damage.*

A wider street-level view, muted serious palette, no flames anywhere. One older masonry
house has fully collapsed into a low rubble pile. Nearby, a mid-rise concrete-frame
building has a crushed upper corner with exposed cracked columns. In the middle
distance a small group of people walk quickly toward open ground — **shown from
behind, backs to the viewer, no visible faces**. Downed power lines lie across part of
the street, away from any people.

### Image 10 of 12 — "Very destructive" (EMS X)
*Meaning: many ordinary well-built buildings collapse.*

A neighbourhood-scale street view: several concrete buildings have collapsed, floor
slabs visibly stacked on each other; between them a few damaged-but-standing structures
remain. A small cluster of survivors stands together at a safe distance from any
rubble, calm and composed. On the horizon, a distant intact citadel-and-minaret
silhouette reminiscent of Erbil anchors the scene in Kurdistan. Documentary, restrained
tone; no human detail within the rubble.

### Image 11 of 12 — "Devastating" (EMS XI)
*Meaning: most well-built buildings collapse.*

An elevated, almost aerial-diagram view over a district: most structures down, a few
isolated heavily-engineered buildings still standing but badly damaged, streets blocked
by debris, a light dust haze over the scene, dry Zagros hills framing the valley.
Deliberately distant and schematic — like a simplified aerial map illustration, not a
close street scene. **No individual human figures** at this framing distance.

### Image 12 of 12 — "Total destruction" (EMS XII)
*Meaning: almost all structures above and below ground heavily damaged or destroyed.*

A landscape-scale, map-like illustration: a flattened settlement in a valley between
golden-brown hills, visible ground fissures running across open terrain. No individual
recognizable buildings — abstract and diagrammatic at this scale, in the spirit of a
simplified relief map. Render it restrained, not as a dramatic centerpiece.

## Part 3 — Set 2: the ten building-damage tiles

These depict **a building's condition after an earthquake** — exterior view, the
building is the subject. No people needed (a small figure for scale is fine at the
higher grades, never near rubble). Same Style Bible, same palette, same world as
Set 1. Within each five-image sequence below, draw **the SAME building** progressing
through worsening damage — same colors, same window layout, same viewpoint — so users
instantly read the sequence as one building getting worse.

### 3.1 The apartment block (multi-storey, concrete frame with block infill)

**Image A1 — no visible damage.**
A calm exterior view of a mid-rise concrete-frame apartment building in a Kurdish town:
clean walls, intact windows, level rooftop water tanks and satellite dishes. Nothing
damaged or out of place.

**Image A2 — hairline cracks.**
The same apartment building; one infill wall panel shows fine hairline cracks in the
plaster near a window corner. Otherwise fully intact — no broken windows, no debris.

**Image A3 — diagonal wall cracks, some fallen plaster.**
The same building with visible diagonal X-shaped cracks across one or two infill wall
panels between the structural columns; a few small pieces of fallen plaster on the
ground beneath. The concrete columns and beams themselves show no cracking — only the
infill walls.

**Image A4 — structural cracking, infill walls failing.**
The same building; one infill panel has partially fallen away, exposing the concrete
frame behind it, and a visible wide crack runs across one structural column near a
beam-column joint. A small rubble pile at the base, no people near it.

**Image A5 — partial collapse.**
The same building with one corner partially collapsed — a floor slab visibly pancaked
or tilted at one level, a buckled column beneath it — while the rest of the building
still stands. Light dust haze, muted palette, no flames, no people.

### 3.2 The house (single-storey, flat-roofed masonry, Iraqi-typical)

**Image H1 — no visible damage.**
A calm exterior view of a single-storey flat-roofed masonry house typical of Kurdistan:
clean walls, intact windows and door, level roofline, courtyard wall with a metal gate,
rooftop water tank. Nothing damaged.

**Image H2 — hairline cracks.**
The same house; one exterior wall shows a fine hairline crack in the plaster near a
window frame. Otherwise fully intact.

**Image H3 — large wall cracks, some fallen masonry.**
The same house with a large diagonal crack across one exterior wall, a small area of
fallen plaster and masonry chunks on the ground beneath it, and one broken window pane.
Roofline and door intact.

**Image H4 — serious wall damage.**
The same house; one exterior wall seriously cracked and partially bulging outward, a
section of the roof-edge parapet broken away and fallen, a modest rubble pile at the
wall's base. The rest of the house stands. No people near the rubble.

**Image H5 — partial or full collapse.**
The same house with one full wall and part of the flat roof collapsed into a low rubble
pile; the remaining walls stand but are clearly unsafe. Muted restrained palette, light
dust haze, no flames, no people in or near the rubble.

## Part 4 — Technical delivery specification

| Requirement | Value |
|---|---|
| Format | **PNG** |
| Canvas | **Square**, minimum 1024×1024 px |
| Background | **Fully transparent** (alpha channel — no white or colored background box; the app layers each image over its own colored tile, so a baked-in background would visibly break every tile) |
| Outlines | Dark and high-contrast — each image must stay readable over BOTH a near-white and a near-black tile color behind it |
| Safe margin | Keep the main subject within the **center ~85%** of the square (the app rounds the tile corners) |
| Text | **None**, in any image |

**Exact filenames (22 files):**

```
level-01.png … level-12.png        (Set 1, in the order given in Part 2)

damage-highrise-01.png … damage-highrise-05.png   (Set 2 apartment block, A1–A5)
damage-lowrise-01.png  … damage-lowrise-05.png    (Set 2 house, H1–H5)
```

## Part 5 — Acceptance checklist (run per image before delivering)

1. ☐ Flat cartoon style, thick outlines, flat colors — no gradients/3D/photo-realism.
2. ☐ Recognizably Kurdistan: flat roofs, concrete-and-block or masonry buildings,
     water tanks, Kurdish dress and domestic detail — nothing Western-suburban.
3. ☐ Same recurring characters and same palette as the rest of the set.
4. ☐ Damage sequence images (Set 2): same building across all five grades of its
     sequence.
5. ☐ Zero injuries, bodies, blood, anguished faces, fire, or smoke.
6. ☐ Zero text, numerals, or watermarks.
7. ☐ Transparent background, subject inside the center 85%.
8. ☐ Still legible when previewed at 120×120 px (squint test: is the one key action —
     stillness / rattling / falling objects / cracks / collapse — obvious at a
     glance?).
9. ☐ The 22 files carry the exact filenames from Part 4.
