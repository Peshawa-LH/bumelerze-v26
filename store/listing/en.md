## Full description (≤4000 chars, both stores)

Bumelerze (بوومەلەرزە — "earthquake" in Kurdish) is the earthquake app built for
Kurdistan.

Earthquake news for Kurdish speakers used to mean rumors on Facebook, a Telegram
forward, or a foreign-language app that doesn't cover the region well. Bumelerze is
different: fast, trustworthy, scientifically correct earthquake information, in your
own language, built by seismic-risk researchers who work on this region every day.

**WHAT YOU GET**

📍 **Live earthquake feed** — Kurdistan first. See recent and historical earthquakes
near you and across the region, with magnitude, depth, distance, and time — big,
clear, and readable the moment you open the app.

🫱 **"Did you feel it?"** — One tap tells us what you felt, in seconds, even on a weak
connection. Every report you send helps build a real, community-owned dataset of how
earthquakes actually feel on the ground in Kurdistan — not borrowed from anyone else.

🗺️ **SHAKEmap** — Bumelerze computes its own scientific intensity maps for earthquakes
in the region, including ones no global service ever maps. Every map shows its data
sources and review status, so you always know how it was made.

📈 **Live sensor** — Turn your phone into a simple seismometer and watch real-time
shaking on-screen, whenever you're curious.

🛡️ **Safety & preparedness** — Plain-language guidance on what to do before, during,
and after an earthquake, written for Kurdistan's homes and streets, not translated
from somewhere else.

**BUILT ON REAL SCIENCE**

Bumelerze isn't a repost of a foreign feed. Its SHAKEmaps are computed by the team's
own tools, its intensity scale follows the EMS-98 standard used by seismologists, its
data sources are always cited, and every map shows whether it's still automatic or has
been reviewed by a scientist. Built by a seismic-risk PhD researcher who works on
Kurdistan and the wider Zagros region professionally.

**KURDISH FIRST**

Bumelerze speaks Sorani (کوردیی ناوەندی), Kurmanji, Arabic, and English — Sorani
first, right-to-left, from the very first screen. This isn't an app translated into
Kurdish after the fact; it's built for Kurdish readers from day one.

Free. No ads. No account required to see earthquakes or send a felt report.
